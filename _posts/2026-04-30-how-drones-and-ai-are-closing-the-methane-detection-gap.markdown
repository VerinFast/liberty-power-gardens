## The Detection Revolution

For years, methane monitoring relied on ground-level sensors and periodic surveys—a method that was slow, expensive, and prone to missing emissions. Today, a new generation of detection technologies is transforming how we find and quantify methane leaks.

## Satellite Imaging and AI

Space-based methane detection is now mature. Satellites equipped with advanced spectrometers can identify methane plumes from orbit, pinpointing sources across entire regions. Machine learning algorithms then analyze these images to detect patterns humans would miss, identifying super-emitter sites in real time.

The scale is remarkable: satellite networks can now monitor thousands of square kilometers simultaneously, identifying methane hotspots with precision that was unimaginable just five years ago.

## Drones and LiDAR

On the ground, autonomous drones equipped with laser-based detection systems (LiDAR) are mapping methane concentrations at high resolution. These systems can quantify emission rates from individual sources—landfills, industrial facilities, wastewater treatment plants—with accuracy that ground-level monitoring simply can't match.

What makes this powerful is the speed and cost. A drone survey that used to take weeks can now be completed in hours. The data is immediate and actionable.

## Closing the Accountability Gap

Until recently, methane emissions were almost impossible to dispute. A facility could claim low emissions while satellite data showed the opposite. Now, independent verification is straightforward. This technology is creating unprecedented transparency.

## From Detection to Solutions

The real value emerges when detection is paired with capture. Knowing where methane is coming from is only half the battle. The next step is deploying capture technologies—biodigestion systems, vent capture, emission controls—right where the data shows they're needed most.

This is where precision matters. Instead of building capture systems everywhere, we can deploy them strategically, based on real emissions data. The result is more efficient, faster payback, and maximum environmental impact.

## The Data-Driven Future

Methane detection technology is maturing fast. The bottleneck is no longer *knowing* where emissions are. It's deciding to act on that knowledge.
